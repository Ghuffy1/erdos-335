# Local inverse alternatives and a subsequential quasi-periodic reduction

Supporting owner proof for #506, 2026-11-10. This packet concerns positive ordinary densities \(\alpha=d(A)\), \(\beta=d(B)\), \(s=d(C)=\alpha+\beta\) with \(C=A+B\) and \(\Delta=b^*(C)-s>0\). It does not assume ordinary residue densities. In the final part both input densities are irrational. The conclusions there concern subsequences. The same finite decomposition across all subsequences is a separate unresolved step.

## Good scales and exact local limits

For any sequence of prefix lengths tending to infinity, choose a rapidly increasing subsequence \((N_k)\). As in [the local compensation lemma](local_deficit_compensation_20261110T2246Z.md), choose a lower scale dominating all three ordinary count errors up to \(3N_k\). Apply [Ackelsberg--Richter, Theorem 8.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12864v1) first to \(1_A\) and then to \(1_B\), using the first pair of bounds as the input bounds of the second application. A scale \((H_k)\) between the final bounds is good for both, tends to infinity, and is \(o(N_k)\). Total A, B, and C counts in every relevant window of length \(H_k+o(H_k)\) have relative densities \(\alpha+o(1),\beta+o(1),s+o(1)\) uniformly.

In the forward orientation, Theorem 9.1 of the same paper supplies for every good start n a highly divisible \(Q_n=(1+o(1))H_k\), a trimmed A window and a trimmed prefix \(B\cap[H_k]\), with \(o(H_k)\) removals and \(o(H_k)\) modular sums outside the actual C window. Adjoin to the prefix side a slowly growing initial segment of B, as in their proof of Theorem 9.1 or the local compensation lemma. The added wrap errors are \(o(H_k)\). Thus for each fixed h all actual B residues mod h are present eventually, and the modulus is eventually a multiple of h. The reversed orientation has the same assertions with A and B interchanged. We also use the following uniform feature of the proof of Theorem 9.1. Its equation (9.5) supplies a good set and modulus independently of the chosen external subset of \([H_k]\). The removal theorem is uniform in that subset. Thus the same reversed good starts can be used for an external A window shifted to \([H_k]\), even when the external window is chosen after the good starts. Dropping a possible element at offset zero changes the approximating input by at most one.

Suppose \(\alpha\) is irrational. The local compensation lemma rules out every fixed positive finite deficit, uniformly over forward good windows. Together with the window cap, the normalized forward modular sums tend uniformly to s. Take any sequence of forward good starts \(n_k\). Form the ultraproduct of the corresponding cyclic groups with their Loeb probability measures, using a nonprincipal ultrafilter. The two trimmed sets and their modular sum are internal. The sum of the internal sets is the internal set of finite sums. Their Loeb masses are \(\alpha,\beta,s\). A finite-index internal subgroup of index h is represented, on an ultrafilter-large set, by the residue subgroup of index h in the finite cyclic groups. The difference in normalized count between the modular sum and the actual C window is \(o(1)\), since their masses agree in the limit and the one-sided difference was \(o(1)\). Residue labels below are absolute integer labels, obtained by adding \(n_k\) back to the A and output coordinates. Their counts are unchanged by this relabeling.

Apply [Griesmer, Proposition 10.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1807.01694v3) to this exact critical Loeb pair. Its three alternatives are periodic output in measure, a quasi-periodic pair, and parallel Bohr intervals in one open-subgroup coset each. The relevant unique-expression and quotient-size facts are Remark 10.3 and Corollary 10.4 of the same paper.

## The periodic alternative is excluded

Suppose the local output is periodic in measure at index h. Write \(S\) for the residue support of the trimmed A window, \(T=R_h(B)\) for the literal B support, and \(R=R_h(A)\). Residue supports of internal sets stabilize along the ultrafilter. In particular \(S\subset R\). Put \(W=S+T\). The Loeb output fills W and has no mass outside W, so \(|W|/h=s\), and the actual C window has the same residue profile.

Let \(T^+\) consist of the residues whose B count in the actual window starting at \(n_k\) has positive normalized ultralimit. Then \(|T^+|/h\ge\beta\). For each fixed \(r\in R\), choose a literal \(a_r\in A\) in that class. Translating the B window by \(a_r\) changes the endpoints by \(O(1)\) and shows \(R+T^+\subset W\). We also have \(S+T\subset W\) by construction. The capacity bound \(|S|/h\ge\alpha\) is strict since \(\alpha\) is irrational. Hence
\[
|W|<|S|+|T^+|.
\]
Apply [the finite completion lemma](lemmas_20261110T2218Z.md) to \(S,T^+,W\). It implies \(R+T\subset S+T^+\subset W\). But \(R+T=R_h(C)\), so \(b^*(C)\le|W|/h=s\), a contradiction.

## The interval alternative gives a global interval pair

In this alternative the trimmed inputs lie in one residue class each modulo h, and their local sum has measure s in one output class. Since the prefix B support equals its literal support at h, B is literally in that one class. If A had an additional literal class, one fixed A representative from it would translate a B window of mass \(\beta\) into an extra output class. The entire actual C window already agrees in measure with the modular core of mass s. This is impossible, so A is literally in one class too.

The local Bohr intervals have positive mass and are inverse images under a measurable surjective character from the subgroup of index h to \(\mathbb T\). Its restriction to every further finite-index subgroup is surjective. Otherwise its image would be a proper finite-index subgroup of the divisible group \(\mathbb T\). The restricted Loeb probability pushes forward to Haar by translation invariance. Hence the two interval preimages, and their full-measure input fillings, have positive measure in every coset of every further fixed residue subgroup. The literal rescaled A and B therefore meet every residue class. The common lattice rescaling gives density sum \(hs<1\) since \(b^*(C)>s\) and \(b^*(C)\le1/h\). Apply [Ackelsberg--Richter, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12864v1) to the rescaled actual pair. Its internal modulus is 1 because the first set meets every residue class, and its zero-density degenerate case is excluded. The resulting actual sets lie literally in parallel closed Bohr interval pullbacks and fill them up to density zero.

## The quasi-periodic alternative and its bounded index

Now assume both \(\alpha,\beta\) are irrational and the interval conclusion does not occur. For the local quasi-periodic alternative at index h, write \(S\subset R=R_h(A)\) and \(T=R_h(B)\) as above, with active residues \(i\in S\), \(j\in T\). The local A window fills the classes \(S\setminus\{i\}\) in measure, while the B prefix fills \(T\setminus\{j\}\). The active relative densities are
\[
x=\alpha h-(|S|-1),\qquad y=\beta h-(|T|-1).
\]
Both are in \((0,1)\) by irrationality. The local output fills \(W\setminus\{u\}\) for \(W=S+T\), \(u=i+j\), and has relative density \(z=x+y<1\) in u. The residue u has the unique expression \((i,j)\) in \(S+T\), and \(|W|=|S|+|T|-1\).

The actual B window has relative count at least \(y-o(1)\) in every class of T, since it has total \(\beta+o(1)\) and is literally supported on T. Fixed translates by A representatives therefore imply \(R+T=W\). In particular
\[
\Delta\le |W|/h-s=(1-z)/h,
\]
so \(h<1/\Delta\). This bound is independent of the chosen prefix subsequence and good starts.

Suppose \(R\ne S\). If W is a proper subset of \(\mathbb Z/h\), coarsen by the stabilizer \(L\) of W in this finite group. Put \(m=|L|\) and \(h'=h/m\). Finite Kneser for each of \(S,T\) and \(R,T\) gives
\[
|W|=|S+L|+|T+L|-m=|R+L|+|T+L|-m.
\]
Thus S and R have the same literal projected support modulo \(h'\). The total structural holes of S and T relative to their L-enlargements equal \(m-1\). Every such hole lies in one of the two coarse active classes. In fact if there were a hole outside those two classes, the holes in them, including their partial h-fibers of missing masses \(1-x,1-y\), would sum to at most \(m-2+(1-x)+(1-y)=m-z<m\) in units of one fine class. The two active coarse input masses would then sum to more than the coarse capacity. Pigeonhole inside that finite-index internal subgroup would fill the coarse active output in measure, contradicting its positive deficiency in u. All nonactive coarse input classes are therefore full in the local model. An alternative expression of the coarse active output using a background input would also fill that output in measure by a fixed translate. This proves the unique-expression property at \(h'\), the projected finite critical identity, and the exact local quasi-periodic description with actual supports on both sides. Because W is proper, \(h'>1\). If both projected supports are singletons, both original sets lie literally in one class modulo \(h'\), and a common lattice rescaling is possible.

A second argument applies if the actual C section at u has a missing residue at some finer modulus. Choose the starts \(n_k\) good for both orientations, which is possible since each good set has size \(N_k-o(N_k)\). Use the active i-section of that forward A window, shifted by \(n_k\), as the external input for the reversed modulated sum theorem. At reversed start \(m=n_k\), its modular sum with the actual B-window j-section is contained up to \(o(H_k)\) in the actual C window at \(2n_k\), in residue u. The latter has relative capacity bounded away from 1 by the fixed missing refinement.

We use this finite consequence of Kneser. If two subsets of growing finite cyclic groups have first relative density tending to an irrational \(x\in(0,1)\) and second lower relative density at least \(1-x\), their sum fills relative density tending to 1. Otherwise a fixed positive deficit gives a bounded-index stabilizer. Passing to a fixed index d, the support capacities of the two sets are each strictly greater than x and \(1-x\), respectively, by irrationality. Their cardinalities of quotient supports therefore sum to at least \(d+1\), and Kneser's formula forces a full sum, a contradiction. The same proof works along the selected ultrafilter.

It follows from the literal C capacity that the relative B-window count in j has ultralimit strictly below \(1-x\). If \(R\ne S\), then \(R,T\) has deficit \(|R|+|T|-|W|\ge2\). The finite Kneser hole formula implies at least this many representations of each output residue, by pigeonhole in a representing stabilizer coset pair. In particular \(u=r+t\) for some \(t\ne j\). The B-window count in t has relative ultralimit strictly greater than \(1+y-(1-x)=z\), by the total B-window mass and the upper capacities of the other classes. A fixed literal A representative in r then puts relative mass greater than z in the actual C u-window at \(n_k\), a contradiction. Thus \(R=S\).

If the literal \(b^*(C)<1\) and W is the whole quotient, there is a missing refinement in the active u-class: each other class is relatively full along the chosen windows, so every finer class there is literally met. Therefore when \(b^*(C)<1\), the two arguments above always give a local quasi-periodic pattern with actual supports on both sides, or a common lattice to divide out.

## Gluing one support-exact local pattern along the selected subsequence

Suppose the quasi-periodic pattern has actual supports \(S=R_h(A)\), \(T=R_h(B)\), and a nonempty background on at least one side. We may choose the reference starts \(n_k=o(N_k)\) good for both orientations before taking the ultraproduct. The preceding arguments apply to those starts. If a coarsening gives both backgrounds empty, it exhibits a common lattice instead. After taking an ordinary subsequence from the ultrafilter, the reference A window and the B prefix have the required full background sections and relative active masses x,y up to \(o(1)\). Their occupied classes are fixed. The original sets satisfy the literal support restrictions at h.

Consider every forward good window. Its A sections in all classes of S have relative mass at least \(x-o(1)>0\) by ordinary total mass and literal support. Combining such a section with a relatively full B prefix section in \(T\setminus\{j\}\) fills the corresponding output class up to \(o(H_k)\). In the reversed orientation, use the reference A window shifted by \(n_k\) as external input. Every B window has relative mass at least \(y-o(1)>0\) in each class of T. Thus its modular sum with a full reference A section in \(S\setminus\{i\}\) fills the corresponding C output class, with window starts shifted by \(n_k\). The shift removes only \(o(N_k)\) start positions from the common range. For all but \(o(N_k)\) starts in \([1,N_k]\), these two conclusions fill every occupied C residue other than u. The relative count in u is then \(z+o(1)\) by the total ordinary window count. This step uses that every output residue other than u has a representation involving a background on at least one side.

The active relative A and B counts also tend to x and y on these good windows. Here is the argument for A. Its local active count is at least \(x-o(1)\) by capacity. If it exceeds \(x+\eta\) along a sequence of good windows, pair its trimmed active section with the actual B prefix active section, of relative mass \(y+o(1)\). Retain the fixed representatives of each literal active B residue at all bounded refinements by the prefix augmentation. The modular sum lies up to \(o(1)\) in the active C window of relative count \(z+o(1)\). Thus the finite active pair has a fixed deficit. At a bounded index d in the rescaled active cyclic group, Kneser gives a periodic core on \(S_2+T_2\) of relative size \((|S_2|+|T_2|-1)/d\), with \(T_2\) the actual support of the active B section at d. The rescaled active C section has upper Buck density strictly above z. This follows from \(b^*(C)>s\), since all other occupied h-classes have full literal profinite closures. Hence there is an extra literal residue of the active sum outside the core. A fixed representative in the active A section and the actual B window, whose active mass is at least \(y-o(1)\), contribute at least \(y-(|T_2|-1)/d-o(1)\) in that extra class. Adding this lower bound to the core gives active C-window relative density at least \(|S_2|/d+y-o(1)\), greater than z by the capacity bound on the active A section. This is impossible. The reversed argument uses the actual A prefix, whose i-count is at least \(x-o(1)\) by capacity, and all its literal active representatives. It proves the B upper bound in the same way. The reversed good set for the actual prefix may be intersected with the shifted good set already used to control C, since both exceptions are \(o(N_k)\).

Now average the window counts over starts in \([1,N_k]\). The window boundary costs \(O(H_k/N_k)\) and the exceptional starts have proportion \(o(1)\). This proves that along the selected prefix subsequence all background A and B classes are relatively full, the active input counts are x and y, and the active output count is \(z=x+y\). The finite quotient identity and unique-expression conditions are literal. This is a subsequential structural reduction at a bounded index, without any initial residue-density assumption.

## Remaining gaps

This packet proves the local exclusion and interval alternatives for irrational \(\alpha\), and the bounded-index quasi-periodic conclusions when both inputs are irrational. Under a support-exact local pattern it proves saturation of the background sections along the selected prefix subsequence. After dividing the maximal common lattice, when the normalized \(b^*(C)<1\) such a support-exact pattern exists on a subsequence of any prescribed sequence of prefix lengths, unless the original pair is already a Bohr interval pair. The normalized \(b^*(C)=1\) case can still have a local quasi-periodic pattern with \(R\supsetneq S\). The argument for a missing finer output residue does not cover it.

Even where subsequential saturation is established, different subsequences may a priori select different moduli and active residues. A global proof must rule out incompatible templates or find a common refinement or coarsening that gives one literal decomposition. When an input density is rational, zero active masses and fixed local deficits also remain possible. No full classification is asserted.
