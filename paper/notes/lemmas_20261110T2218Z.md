# Erdős 335 with a sumset that is periodic up to density zero

Proved supporting result for #506, 2026-11-10. Here d is ordinary natural density, and \(E\sim F\) means \(d(E\triangle F)=0\). For a subset S of the residue group modulo q let \(P_q(S)=\{n\ge 1:n\bmod q\in S\}\). Write \(R_q(E)\) for the actual residue support. The quantity \(\inf_q |R_q(E)|/q\) is upper Buck density, denoted \(b^*(E)\). A periodic cover has this density as its infimum. See François Hennecart, [Kneser's theorem for upper Buck density](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13275v2).

## A finite-group lemma

Let S,T be nonempty subsets of a finite abelian group. Suppose \(S+T\subset U\) and \(|U|<|S|+|T|\). Define
\[
S'=\{x:x+T\subset U\},\qquad T'=\{y:S+y\subset U\}.
\]
Then \(S'+T'\subset S+T\).

Proof. Set \(V=S+T\) and \(H=\operatorname{Stab}(V)\), with \(h=|H|\). Finite Kneser gives
\[
|V|\ge |S+H|+|T+H|-h.
\]
Since these three cardinalities are multiples of h and \(|V|<|S|+|T|\le |S+H|+|T+H|\), equality holds. Put \(u_S=|S+H|-|S|\), \(u_T=|T+H|-|T|\) and \(e=|S|+|T|-|V|=h-u_S-u_T>0\). The room \(|U\setminus V|\) is strictly less than e.

Let bars denote images modulo H. The stabilizer of \(\bar V\) is trivial and \(|\bar V|=|\bar S|+|\bar T|-1\). If \(x\notin S+H\), adding \(\bar x\) to \(\bar S\) must enlarge \(\bar S+\bar T\). Otherwise finite Kneser for the same aperiodic sumset would require at least \(|\bar S|+|\bar T|\) elements. Thus there is an occupied T coset whose translate by x lies outside V. That coset has at least \(h-u_T\ge e\) elements of T, so it cannot be mapped into \(U\setminus V\). This proves \(S'\subset S+H\), and symmetry gives \(T'\subset T+H\). Their sum lies in V.

The strict hypothesis is essential. In \(\mathbb Z/5\), \(S=\{0,1\}, T=\{0,2\}, U=\{0,1,2,3\}\), \(3+T\subset U\) and \(S+1\subset U\), yet \(3+1\notin U\). Here \(|U|=|S|+|T|\).

## Exact criterion for two summands that are periodic up to density zero

Suppose \(A\sim P_q(S)\) and \(B\sim P_q(T)\), with nonempty S,T. Let
\[
U=(R_q(A)+T)\cup(S+R_q(B)),\qquad
D=((A\setminus P_q(S))+(B\setminus P_q(T)))\setminus P_q(U).
\]
Then \(A+B\) contains all of \(P_q(U)\) except a density-zero subset, and \((A+B)\setminus P_q(U)=D\) exactly.

For each class in \(R_q(A)+T\), take an actual representative \(a\in A\) of the first residue and translate the relatively full B section of the second residue. This covers that output class up to density zero. Do the symmetric operation for \(S+R_q(B)\). Conversely a sum outside \(P_q(U)\) can use neither a residue in S from A nor a residue in T from B, giving the stated identity.

It follows that the exact ordinary-density equality holds if and only if
\[
|U|=|S|+|T| \quad\hbox{and}\quad \overline d(D)=0.
\]
Indeed if \(|U|>|S|+|T|\), the covered residue classes already exceed the desired density. If \(|U|<|S|+|T|\), the finite-group lemma applies since \(S+T\subset U\), \(R_q(A)+T\subset U\), and \(S+R_q(B)\subset U\). Thus \(R_q(A)+R_q(B)\subset S+T\subset U\), so D is empty and the sumset has density \(|U|/q\), which is too small. If the sizes agree, the contribution of D must vanish and that condition also ensures existence of the desired density.

For ultimately periodic summands D is finite. That specialization is the finite-prefix theorem already proved in the July 2026 [Erdős Problem a Day working report](https://www.erdosproblemaday.com/report/335). The density-zero fringe condition is essential in the more general theorem. The earlier digital construction in lemmas_20261110T2006Z.md exhibits its full range of effects.

## A converse reduction when the sumset is periodic up to density zero

Assume \(d(A)=\alpha>0,d(B)=\beta>0,d(A+B)=s=\alpha+\beta\). Suppose \(A+B\sim P_q(U_0)\) and \(b^*(A+B)>s\).

Let S contain those q-classes in which A has positive upper density, and T those in which B has positive upper density. A outside \(P_q(S)\) has density zero, and B outside \(P_q(T)\) likewise. Thus \(\alpha\le |S|/q\), \(\beta\le|T|/q\) and \(|S|+|T|\ge |U_0|=qs\).

Every actual residue of A added to any residue in T must lie in \(U_0\). Otherwise translating the positive-upper-density section of B by one actual element of A would create positive upper density outside \(P_q(U_0)\). Similarly \(S+R_q(B)\subset U_0\).

If \(|S|+|T|>|U_0|\), the finite-group lemma implies \(R_q(A)+R_q(B)\subset U_0\). This would give \(b^*(A+B)\le |U_0|/q=s\), contrary to the strict assumption. Consequently \(\alpha=|S|/q\) and \(\beta=|T|/q\). Both sets therefore fill their respective periodic cores up to density zero, and the exact criterion above applies. Its U equals \(U_0\) since \(U\subset U_0\) and their sizes agree.

In particular finitely many fixed translates of the original summands already cover the sumset up to density zero in this branch. Take one representative of every actual q-residue on each side. The proof uses upper densities to find the cores and does not presume that individual residue-class densities exist.

## Explicit failure of sufficiency for the degenerate inverse conclusion

Theorem 1.4 of Ackelsberg and Richter gives a necessary structural alternative. Its case (2) alone cannot be used as a converse. [Primary theorem](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12864v1#Thmtheorem1.4)

Let \(D_0=\bigcup_{k\ge1}[k^2,k^2+k)\cap\mathbb N\). It has ordinary density \(1/2\) by summing the block lengths, with discrepancy \(O(\sqrt N)\). For every fixed integer t, the symmetric difference \(D_0\triangle(D_0+t)\) has density zero because only \(O(\sqrt N)\) block boundaries occur up to N.

Let \(P\) be the positive set of \(j^2+1\) for \(j\ge0\), with the very sparse residue-complete set Z from the previous proof adjoined. It has density zero and meets every residue class. The sumset \(D_0+P\) is cofinite. For large n write \(n-1=k^2+u\) with \(0\le u\le 2k\), and take \(j=\lfloor\sqrt u\rfloor\). Then \(0\le u-j^2\le 2\sqrt{2k}+1<k\) for all sufficiently large k, so \(n-(j^2+1)\in D_0\).

Now set \(A=2D_0\) and \(B=(2\mathbb N-1)\cup 2P\). They have densities \(1/4\) and \(1/2\), and B meets every residue class. With h=2, A is contained in the exceptional even class, B is full on the odd classes and has density zero on the even class, and both A and B are invariant up to density zero under fixed even translations. Yet \(A+B\) is cofinite, with density 1. Odd sums come from one fixed a and all odd positive integers. Even sums follow from \(D_0+P\). Thus the case (2) invariance and residue data permit genuine extra density when both summands in a representation can vary.
