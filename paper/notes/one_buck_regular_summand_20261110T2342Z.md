# A summand whose upper Buck density equals its ordinary density

Supporting owner proof for #506, 2026-11-10. Suppose \(A,B\subset\mathbb N\) have positive ordinary densities \(\alpha,\beta\), \(C=A+B\) has \(d(C)=s=\alpha+\beta<b^*(C)\), and \(b^*(A)=\alpha\). Then at least one of A and B is periodic in density. The [periodic-summand reduction](periodic_summand_exact_reduction_20261110T2330Z.md) applies. No residue-density hypothesis on B is needed.

Let \(\Omega=\widehat{\mathbb Z}\) with Haar probability \(\mu\), and \(F_E\) be the literal profinite closure of E. Then \(b^*(E)=\mu(F_E)\). Along any sequence of prefixes, pass to a diagonal subsequence on which all B and C residue counts converge. These define measures dominated by \(\mu\), with densities \(b,c\in[0,1]\) and masses \(\beta,s\). For example the corresponding values on a clopen q-coset are the limits of normalized counts in that coset, and domination and consistency give the bounded martingale densities. The same limiting measure for A is necessarily \(1_{F_A}\mu\). Every subsequential limit is dominated by \(\mu\), supported on \(F_A\), and has mass \(\alpha=\mu(F_A)\), so it equals that measure. Thus the A residue limits already exist.

For every literal \(a\in A\), the shifted profile \(b(\cdot-a)\) is at most c almost everywhere. This is the limit of the count inequality from \(a+B\subset C\), with a bounded endpoint error. The same reasoning gives \(1_{F_A}(\cdot-b_0)\le c\) almost everywhere for each literal \(b_0\in B\). Translation is continuous in \(L^1(\mu)\), so these inequalities extend to every \(a\in F_A\) and \(b_0\in F_B\).

Use convolution with the Haar probability of \(q_n\Omega\), \(q_n=n!\), as conditional expectation. Define \(a^\circ(x)=1\) at points of \(F_A\) where the convolutions of \(1_{F_A}\) tend to 1, and zero elsewhere. Define \(b^\circ(y)\) as the limit of the convolutions of b on \(F_B\) where it exists, and zero elsewhere. These versions equal \(1_{F_A},b\) almost everywhere. The translated inequalities survive convolution at all points. Therefore at any \(\gamma\) where the c averages converge, and every literal profinite representation \(\gamma=x+y\) with \(x\in F_A,y\in F_B\),
\[
c(\gamma)\ge\max\{a^\circ(x),b^\circ(y)\}=\min\{1,a^\circ(x)+b^\circ(y)\}.
\]
The last equality uses the 0-or-1 value of \(a^\circ\).

In \(\Omega\times\mathbb T\), form the two vertical interval sets from these height functions, as in [the compact transfer](residue_density_compact_transfer_20261110T2227Z.md). Retain the singleton at height zero over every literal carrier point. Their masses are \(\alpha,\beta\), and their sumset mass is at most \(\int c=s\) by the displayed inequality and the fiber formula. Its projection is exactly \(F_A+F_B=F_C\). Strict compact Kneser would make the sumset literally periodic by an open subgroup containing the circle, giving mass at least \(b^*(C)>s\). Hence its mass is s. Its fiber lengths equal c almost everywhere.

Apply [Griesmer's compact inverse theorem](https://arxiv.org/html/1112.6403v3). The periodic and extendible alternatives are excluded by the same strict literal Buck gap, as in the compact transfer. The interval alternative is also impossible. The A model contains whole circle fibers above a positive-measure set, whereas the interval character on an open subgroup \(h\Omega\times\mathbb T\) must be nontrivial on the circle, since a continuous character of the profinite factor has finite image. The circle fibers cannot then lie in a proper arc pullback. The arcs must be proper because both masses are positive and their sum fits in one subgroup coset.

Consequently the model has a finite quasi-periodic step. All background classes on both sides are full in the limiting profiles. The active expression is unique and the exact finite quotient identity follows by comparing masses. The new active pair of model sets again consists of vertical interval sets with A height 0 or 1. Its sum mass is the sum of its active input masses. Its difference between literal projection mass and sumset mass is the former difference multiplied by the subgroup index. Since each step has index at least 2, and the normalized difference cannot exceed 1, the iteration terminates after finitely many steps at one zero-mass active input. The combined modulus is at most \(1/(b^*(C)-s)\). At that stage the zero-side profile is the indicator of finitely many full background classes, since the active mass is zero and all earlier backgrounds stay full after refinement.

If A is the zero side along any chosen subsequence, its already-unique ordinary profile shows that A is periodic in density. If A is not periodic in density, every subsequence instead has a further subsequence whose B profile is the indicator of a finite periodic template of modulus at most \(1/(b^*(C)-s)\). At a common multiple of these bounded moduli, the possible B prefix profiles form a finite set. The successive prefix-count differences tend to zero, so the cluster set is a singleton by the same elementary connectedness argument as in [the irrational-density theorem](irrational_input_classification_20261110T2322Z.md). This gives an actual ordinary B profile of a periodic indicator and hence B is periodic in density.

The transfer inequality here uses only fixed translates and the 0-or-1 profile on one side. It does not import the sectionwise integer Kneser bound for arbitrary subsequential densities of the other side.
