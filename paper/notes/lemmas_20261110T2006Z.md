## 1. A purely local enclosure cap

For E subset N write R_m(E)={e mod m:e in E} for its ACTUAL support, including isolated elements. Put
rho_m(A,B)=|R_m(A)+R_m(B)|/m, and sigma(A,B)=inf_{m>=1}rho_m(A,B).

These are not residue-density supports. Exactly R_m(A+B)=R_m(A)+R_m(B). Consequently upper_d(A+B)<=sigma. If C is ultimately Q-periodic, with k full tail classes, then sigma(C)=d(C)=k/Q: at modulus Ql the full tail occupies kl classes, and the finitely many exceptional elements occupy at most M additional classes, so rho_{Ql}(C)<=k/Q+M/(Ql); the reverse bound is automatic.

Let d(A)=alpha>0,d(B)=beta>0 and s=alpha+beta<=1. Then:
* if sigma<=s, d(A+B) exists and equals sigma;
* in particular sigma=s is a sufficient criterion for the target equality;
* if the target equality holds, sigma>=s.

Proof. If sigma<s, upper_d(C)<s and Kneser's periodicity alternative applies; the preceding paragraph gives d(C)=sigma. If sigma=s but lower_d(C)<s, the same alternative would give lower_d(C)=sigma=s, a contradiction. Thus lower_d(C)>=s>=upper_d(C). This is a diagnostic reduction, not a characterization of the remaining sigma>s branch.

## 2. Complete boundary alpha+beta=1, as a Kneser corollary

For ANY A,B with existing positive ordinary densities alpha+beta=1, d(A+B) exists. It equals1 if and only if
R_m(A)+R_m(B)=Z/mZ for every m>=1.

Equivalently, failure of equality occurs exactly when there exist q>=2, g in G=Z/qZ, and a nonempty proper S subset G such that
A subset {n:n mod q in S},
B subset {n:n mod q in G\(g-S)}.
The given density sum1 forces alpha=|S|/q and beta=(q-|S|)/q. Thus irrational alpha automatically implies equality on this boundary.

Proof. If lower_d(C)=1, the conclusion is immediate. Otherwise Kneser gives ultimate periodicity with density<1. A missing tail class has only finitely many exceptional members of C; refining its modulus by a sufficiently large factor produces an ACTUALLY empty residue class. Hence some R_q(C) omits g. Set S=R_q(A), T=R_q(B). Since S and g-T are disjoint, |S|+|T|<=q. On the other hand alpha<=|S|/q and beta<=|T|/q, so the density sum1 forces equality throughout, including T=G\(g-S). Conversely such literal containments omit g from all sums and prevent density1.

The containments and density equalities show that A and B miss only density-zero subsets of their periodic templates. Every occupied q-section is therefore relatively full. For any r in S+T choose i in S,j in T with i+j=r mod q. The relative section sets have density1, so their sumset is cofinite by the elementary pigeonhole argument (two subsets of [0,n] with cardinalities summing above n+1 must contain a complementary pair). Therefore C is ultimately exactly the periodic template S+T.

More explicitly, if H=Stab(S), then G\(S+T)=g+H: a missing u satisfies T=G\(u-S), equivalently u-S=g-S, equivalently u-g in H. Thus C ultimately omits exactly one residue class modulo h=q/|H|, and d(C)=1-1/h. All finite exceptions to A or B are retained by the literal containments. These boundary conclusions are classical deductions from Kneser; no novelty claim.

## 3. A density-preserving digital transform

Let N0={0,1,...}. Define f(k) by moving binary digit i of k to digit2i:
f(k)=sum_i epsilon_i(k)4^i.
Let X={f(k):k in N0}. For K subset N0 put Y_K={2f(j):j in K}. If d(K)=tau exists, then
d(X)=d(Y_K)=0, and d(X+Y_K)=tau.

Proof. The binary even and odd digit positions are disjoint. Every n>=0 has a UNIQUE decomposition n=f(k)+2f(j), and f is increasing. For N>=1 let
w_j(N)=#{x in X:x<=N-2f(j)}.
The weights are nonnegative and decreasing in j, vanish eventually, and sum_j w_j(N)=N+1. Also w_0(N)=O(sqrt(N)), since there are at most2sqrt(N) possible even-bit numbers up to N.

Write E(j)=sum_{i=0}^j(1_K(i)-tau)=o(j+1). Summation by parts gives the counting error
sum_j(1_K(j)-tau)w_j = sum_j E(j)(w_j-w_{j+1}).
For j>=J, |E(j)|<=epsilon(j+1); hence that tail is bounded by epsilon sum_j(j+1)(w_j-w_{j+1})=epsilon(N+1). The finitely many j<J contribute O_J(w_0)=o(N). Divide by N+1 and let epsilon tend0. Thinness of X,Y follows from the same O(sqrt(N)) count.

For every tau in[0,1] a K of density tau exists, for example the mechanical sequence
1_K(j)=floor((j+1)tau)-floor(j tau).
The construction is a direct binary digit-splitting argument, akin to classical thin additive-basis constructions; its full proof is included and no novelty is asserted.

## 4. Force all ACTUAL residue supports to agree without changing these densities

Enumerate all (m,r), m>=1,0<=r<m, by j=m(m-1)/2+r+1, and put
z_j=2^{j^2}+((r-2^{j^2}) mod m).
Let Z={z_j}. It meets every residue class for every modulus, but |Z cap[0,N]|=O(sqrt(log N)). Thus Z has density0.

Set X*=X union Z and Y_K*=Y_K union Z. They are still zero-density and both meet every residue class. Their sumset differs from X+Y_K only by subsets of (X+Z) union (Z+Y_K) union (Z+Z). Up to N these have at most O(sqrt(N log N))+O(log N)=o(N) elements. Therefore
d(X*+Y_K*)=tau.

Now fix ANY q>=3 and define literal subsets of positive integers
A=qN union {qx+1:x in X*},
B_tau=qN union {qy+1:y in Y_K*}.
For EVERY tau:
d(A)=d(B_tau)=1/q,
and the exact sumset is
{qk:k>=2} union {qk+1:k>=1} union {qz+2:z in X*+Y_K*}.
The first two pieces come from the periodic cores and cross terms; 0 in X ensures all qk+1,k>=1. The third piece is disjoint because q>=3. Hence
d(A+B_tau)=(2+tau)/q.

It follows that the target equality holds in this family exactly for tau=0. All summands differ from the SAME periodic core qN by density-zero sets, while sumset densities range continuously from2/q to3/q.

Even stronger, for every modulus m, with g=gcd(q,m), the ACTUAL residue supports of both summands are identically
{r mod m:r=0 or1 mod g},
independently of tau. All their residue-class densities are also identical: density1/lcm(q,m) for r=0 mod g, and0 otherwise. This follows from the qN core and thinness of the added sets. These are actual ordinary densities, not merely subsequence densities.

For every member of this family sigma(A,B_tau)=3/q: at modulus m the sum support is the image of {0,1,2} modulo g, whose normalized size is at least3/q, and modulus q attains it. Thus even the entire joint list of marginal residue densities and actual supports, and the cap sigma, cannot distinguish equality from nonequality in the remaining sigma>s branch. Both summands have difference-gcd1 (the core differences include q and an exceptional point gives a difference coprime to q), so a common-lattice division does not remove this obstruction.

This DOES NOT classify all equality pairs, refute the original request, or establish that a more detailed structural classification is impossible. It defeats only the proposed classification by all marginal profinite residue data.
