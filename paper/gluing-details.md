## Hypotheses and finite labels

The relevant case has ordinary positive alpha,beta, ordinary C density s=alpha+beta<b*(C), and 0<min(delta_A,delta_B)<=Delta. It borrows from the earlier candidate that every common good-window ultralimit has at least one actual periodic input profile, and from its finite reductions the bounded modulus and precise strict-base or saturated baseline structure. I do not infer this local premise from ordinary shift invariance alone. The zero Buck-gap and both-large-gap branches remain their separate earlier theorems.

At common M, a saturated periodic A mask P determines the B mask uniquely. Indeed the fixed A baseline U has size sM and is full. The positive B support T satisfies |T|>=beta M and R_M(A)+T subset U. If |T|>beta M, the strict finite completion lemma for P,T,U implies the whole literal output support in U, contradicting the strict Buck gap. Equality of cardinalities forces full B on T. Reapplying with T enlarged to its completion gives uniqueness. The symmetric fact holds for B. Both periodic profiles cannot have two strict baselines; the opposite-baseline saturation was checked in the earlier bounded review and uses actual literal residue representatives. Thus the A-mask, B-mask, and both-saturated labels exhaust the locally admissible cases and are mutually exclusive. They retain actual support, not just the positive profile supports.

## Addition closure of a label

For a target of strict B label Q at t+u, its B literal coarse support is the full background T1 plus the unique sparse class j. If source B at u has zero j-mass, its fixed total |T1|/h forces exactly Q, including all fine residues inside those full coarse classes. Its label is already the target label (the baseline of Q is strict). Otherwise choose one fine v above j with b_v(u)>0. Same source label guarantees either A at t is periodic or B at u is periodic; hence in (2) every positive source A entry above active class i produces a FULL target fine entry at r+v. By A's literal support and total, the source active mass is at least x. The number of these distinct full target fine classes divided by M is at least that mass. They are inside target active u=i+j of mass exactly x. Thus all inequalities are equalities, source active entries are 0 or 1, other coarse source A classes are full, and target C active fine profile is exactly their shift by v. The strict-base local fixed-translate comparison says this is also the target A active profile shifted by a fixed literal representative of that same fine v; such a point exists from the positive source mass. Hence the source A profile and target A profile are the same periodic P. The target B Q has strict baseline and both target inputs periodic, so w_A(P)=s. Saturated-mask uniqueness then forces source B at t to be Q, contradiction if the common source label was different. In particular no converse comparison of the varying A/B windows is assumed. The swapped strict A case uses symmetry together with interchanging the source times.

For a both-saturated target, after the earlier bounded coarsening each literal input has one extra class outside its periodic mask; their sum is absent from the target C mask. If a corresponding source input has zero mass in that extra class, its total forces the same periodic target mask, whose saturated baseline forces the full target label by uniqueness. Otherwise if both source labels are a common different label, choose positive fine entries from the A and B extra classes; (2) forces positive C mass in the forbidden coarse output class. Thus closure of a label under addition holds for almost every pair of its times in every target case.

## Measure and ordinary-prefix closure

For a positive-measure label choose a bounded positive-measure subset. Its indicator self-convolution is continuous (bounded indicator and L1 translation continuity), nonnegative and has positive integral. Fubini from the a.e. addition implication forces it to vanish a.e. outside the label, so the label contains some interval (a,b), 0<a<b, up to null sets. Iterating with that interval yields (ka,kb) up to null sets, and those overlap for all large k. Any two positive-measure labels would both contain a tail up to null sets. With finitely many labels partitioning a.e., exactly one occurs a.e.

Every macro extraction then has a fixed periodic indicator on at least one side for a.e. t, and integrating t in [0,1] gives that side's prefix limit. The finite closed slices of the joint actual prefix cluster set are disjoint: the sole cross-kind intersection would have two periodic prefix profiles with both baselines strict, ruled out by the finite prefix saturation argument with the actual C ordinary total. Bounded prefix vectors have successive increments O(M/N); a cluster set split into two positively separated nonempty closed parts would require infinitely many late steps out of their neighborhoods, contradiction. Hence one slice is fixed throughout the cluster set; one summand really converges to its 0-or-1 periodic profile in ordinary residue density and thus is periodic up to density zero. This does not claim the other side is also periodic, nor that infinite sparse translates are automatically harmless.
